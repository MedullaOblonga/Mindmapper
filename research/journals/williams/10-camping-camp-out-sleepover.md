Life on the ISS:

Well, you probably know this last week was EVA week. Lots of last minute preps to get this ready - from charging suit, helmet light, PGT batteries, to scrubbing METOX for CO 2 removal, to arranging tools, and reviewing last minute changes to our procedures. The airlock preparations are sort of cool since it involves lots of tools and hardware!

In order to “go out the door” safely we go through a pretty thorough process called pre-breathe. This is the process of flushing all the nitrogen out of our systems by breathing higher concentrations of oxygen. The reason this is important to EVA (spacewalks) is that we operate at a lower pressure than ambient , in the space suit; about a 4 psi differential from essentially the vacuum of space. As we decrease our overall pressure from ambient, the air bubbles, specifically nitrogen bubbles in the blood, have the potential to expand. This can cause serious pain and other debilitating effects commonly referred to as decompression sickness (DCS) or “the bends.” The diving community knows all about this problem. But the difference with diving is that it would occur as the diver is coming to the surface. The diver can decrease their descent rate, descend to shrink the bubble, or in the worst case get medical attention once at the surface. For us, the problem would occur as we were depressurizing the airlock to open the hatch to go outside as our suit was regulating to maintain the 4 psi differential. That depress rate is set by a pump for the most part, and once we are at vacuum, we can’t get medical care to help us out. So, in this line of work, we need to try and eliminate the occurrence of “the bends” as best we can.

There are 3 methods we use to purge the nitrogen out of our blood to prevent “the bends.” The methods are pretty complicated in reality and a ton of research and data were needed to establish these “protocols.” But, in general they are:

Pre-breathe - Get in the space suit 4 hours early and hang out breathing 100% O 2 before opening the hatch and going outside.

Exercise - Wearing a mask breathing 100% O 2 , exercise on the bike for about 15 minutes doing an interval workout using both arms and legs to accelerate that exchange of O 2 for air in the blood.

Camp-out - Sleep overnight in the airlock at a decreased pressure with an increased O 2 concentration so that the O 2 level in the blood increases as you sleep. Get up in the morning, get on a mask of 100% O 2 to re-pressurize to ambient - to use the bathroom one last time before the EVA - and to let 3rd crewperson into the airlock to help us get in the suit.
